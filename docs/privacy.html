Privacy

Last updated 23 September 2026

OzTrax Recon has no account, no sign-in, and no server of mine behind it. What it records about your driving - your trail, your saved tracks, your waypoints, your favourites, your settings - is written to your phone and stays on your phone. I never see it. Nobody else does either, unless you export a GPX file and send it somewhere yourself.

When you have signal, the app does ask other people's map and search services for things, and those requests tell them roughly where you are. That is what makes a map draw and a search answer. Every one of them is named below.

With no signal, nothing leaves the phone at all, and most of the app still works. That is the point of it.

What this covers

The OzTrax Recon app for Android, and the web app it runs, published at deathcalibergaming.github.io/oztrax4x4/. They are the same thing: the Android app is a window onto that page.

What stays on your phone

All of this is held in this app's own storage on your phone, and in no other place:

  • The trail you are laying down now, and every track you have saved, including its GPS fixes, times, speeds and altitudes.
  • Waypoints you drop, and what you called them.
  • Favourites, your home position if you set one, and your last known position.
  • Settings: text size, which kinds of place and which fuel grades you want shown, which pins you have hidden.
  • Everything you have downloaded for offline use - a state's map, addresses, roads and the POI pack - and which states you downloaded them for.

None of it is uploaded, backed up to me, or sent anywhere by the app. It goes when you delete it, and when you uninstall the app.

Exporting a track writes a GPX file into your phone's downloads. Where that file goes afterwards is up to you.

Each of those is an ordinary web request, so on top of what is listed the service also sees your phone's IP address and the browser's user agent string, and may keep its own log of it. What it does with that is covered by its policy, not this one. I have no arrangement with any of them: the app is one more visitor.

The day's fuel prices come from the app's own address, as a single file for the whole country, so asking for them says nothing about where you are.

Nothing in that list goes to me. I have no server, no database and no log of you.

What the app does not do

  • No account and no sign-in. The app never asks for your name, your email address or a password, because it has nowhere to put them.
  • No analytics and no advertising. No usage tracking, no crash reporting, no advertising ID, no measurement of any kind. There is no third-party code in it for that - the whole app is one file, and you can read it.
  • No cookies, and nothing that follows you between apps or websites.
  • Nothing sold and nothing shared. Not to data brokers, not to advertisers, not to anyone. There is nothing held to sell.
  • No camera, microphone, contacts, calendar, call log, messages or files touched or asked for.

Permissions the app asks for

Location

To put you on the map, lay the trail, count the distance, and search around you. It is used only while the app is open on the screen: it does not follow you in the background, and the phone stops handing out fixes when the app is not in front. You can refuse it, or withdraw it later in Android's settings - the map still works, you just will not be on it.

Keeping the screen awake

While a recording is running, so the phone does not go dark mid-track. It reads nothing.

Speaking the turns

If you switch the navigation voice on, the app asks the phone's own text-to-speech to say the turn it is already showing you. It uses only a voice stored on the phone: if the phone offers nothing but voices that synthesise over the internet, the app refuses them and stays silent, and the line under the switch tells you so. Nothing about where you are or where you are going is sent anywhere to be spoken.

Storage

The app asks the phone to treat its own storage as worth keeping, so Android does not quietly clear your downloaded maps to reclaim space. Its own storage only - it cannot see anything else on the phone.

How long anything is kept, and how to be rid of it

Everything is kept until you remove it. At any time you can:

  • Delete a track or a waypoint in the app.
  • Empty the tile and POI caches under Storage in the menu.
  • Delete a whole state's downloaded data in Download A State.
  • Clear the lot: Android Settings → Apps → OzTrax Recon → Storage. In a browser, clear site data for the app's address.
  • Uninstall the app, which takes all of it with it.

And the other way round, if you would rather keep it: Storage → Back Up writes your tracks, waypoints, favourites and settings to a single file on your phone, which Restore reads back — on this phone, another one, or after a reinstall. The file is written to your device and goes nowhere near me or anybody else unless you send it somewhere yourself. Your downloaded maps are not in it; they come from the server again.

Because I hold nothing about you, there is nothing for me to show you, correct or delete on request. It is all in your hands, on your phone.

Children

The app is not aimed at children, and collects nothing from anybody whatever their age.

If this changes

The app is moving to its own web address before long, and the list of services above will change as more of the map moves onto the phone. When that happens, this page and the date at the top of it change with it. The current version is always the one at this address, and it is stored on the phone with the app, so it can be read with no signal.
